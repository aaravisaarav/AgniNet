Official Wildfire Precautions & Agency Protocols

Guidance from World Health Organization (WHO) & International Civil Defence Organisation (ICDO)

World Health Organization (WHO) — Wildfire Smoke & Health Protection

Wildfire smoke contains hazardous fine particulate matter (PM2.5), carbon monoxide, and toxic gases that penetrate deep into the lungs and bloodstream.

1. Respiratory Protection (N95 / FFP2)

If you must go outdoors in smoky air, wear a well-fitted N95, KN95, or FFP2 respirator. Standard cloth or surgical masks do not filter PM2.5 smoke particles.

2. Create an Indoor Clean-Air Room

Keep all windows, doors, and vents tightly closed. Run an air conditioner on recirculate mode or use a portable HEPA air purifier. Avoid burning candles or frying food indoors.

3. Protect Vulnerable Groups

Children, older adults, pregnant individuals, and people with asthma, COPD, or heart disease should stay indoors, keep rescue inhalers accessible, and seek medical care if chest tightness or wheezing occurs.

4. Hydration & Safe Re-Entry

Drink plenty of clean water to soothe irritated airways. After a fire passes, wet down ash before sweeping and wear gloves, goggles, and a respirator during cleanup.

International Civil Defence Organisation (ICDO) — Emergency Response & Evacuation

When a wildfire starts nearby, rapid civil-defence discipline saves lives. Never wait until flames are visible to prepare evacuation.

1. Immediate Evacuation Readiness

Follow official civil-defence evacuation orders immediately. Park your vehicle facing outward with windows closed, fuel tank full, and keep two alternate escape routes mapped.

2. Shut Off Utilities & Flammable Fuels

Turn off natural gas, LPG cylinders, and propane mains before leaving. Move flammable furniture away from windows and clear dry leaves within 10 meters of your home.

3. ICDO Emergency Go-Bag

Pack drinking water (3L/person), IDs & medical prescriptions, N95 masks, flashlight, power bank, battery radio, cotton/wool long-sleeve clothing, and sturdy closed shoes.

4. If Trapped by Approaching Fire

Do not try to outrun flames uphill. Shelter inside a solid brick/concrete building or a cleared, fuel-free area near water; stay low to the floor where air is cooler and cleaner, and call local emergency services.
