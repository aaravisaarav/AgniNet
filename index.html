Official Wildfire Precautions & Agency Protocols

Guidance from World Health Organization (WHO) & International Civil Defence Organisation (ICDO)

World Health Organization (WHO) — Wildfire Smoke & Health Protection

Wildfire smoke contains hazardous fine particulate matter (PM2.5), carbon monoxide, and toxic gases that penetrate deep into the lungs and bloodstream.

1. Respiratory Protection (N95 / FFP2)

Wear a well-fitted N95, KN95, or FFP2 respirator outdoors. Standard cloth or surgical masks do not filter PM2.5 smoke particles.

2. Indoor Clean-Air Room

Seal all windows and doors. Run AC on recirculate mode or a portable HEPA purifier. Avoid burning candles or frying food indoors.

3. Protect Vulnerable Groups

Children, older adults, pregnant individuals, and people with asthma or heart disease should stay indoors and keep rescue inhalers accessible.

4. Hydration & Safe Re-Entry

Drink plenty of clean water. After a fire passes, wet down ash before sweeping and wear gloves, goggles, and a respirator during cleanup.

International Civil Defence Organisation (ICDO) — Emergency Response & Evacuation

Never wait until flames are visible to prepare evacuation. Follow civil-defence discipline early.

1. Immediate Evacuation Readiness

Follow official evacuation orders immediately. Park vehicles facing outward with windows closed and keep two alternate escape routes mapped.

2. Shut Off Utilities & Fuels

Turn off natural gas, LPG cylinders, and propane mains before leaving. Clear dry leaves within 10 meters of your home.

3. ICDO Emergency Go-Bag

Pack water (3L/person), IDs, prescriptions, N95 masks, flashlight, power bank, battery radio, and cotton/wool long-sleeve clothing.

4. If Trapped by Approaching Fire

Do not outrun flames uphill. Shelter inside a solid brick/concrete structure or cleared area near water; stay low to the floor and call emergency services.

Integrated Global Wildfire & Climate Intelligence Network

AgniNet synthesizes satellite thermal detections with international agency bulletins and country-level news reports:

  • Global Fire Management Hub (GFMH — FAO/UNEP): Integrated fire management knowledge and global wildfire risk frameworks.
  • World Meteorological Organization (WMO): Global fire-weather indices, drought/heatwave advisories, and wind-driven smoke transport alerts.
  • Global Fire Monitoring Center (GFMC): Real-time global wildland fire monitoring and early warning networks.
  • International Civil Defence Organisation (ICDO): Civil protection coordination, evacuation alerts, and emergency field safety protocols.
  • World Health Organization (WHO): Smoke PM2.5 health advisories, respiratory protection, and public health emergency guidance.
